Quantum annealing

Energy-only adiabaticity in a gapless anneal: finite-size TFIM simulations at the critical endpoint

20 qubits pennylane-lightning ~150 min on CPU
Motivation

The seed question asks whether a gapless adiabatic procedure can still guarantee small final energy error even when final-state fidelity is not close to one. A tractable way to probe this is the 1D transverse-field Ising model (TFIM), whose thermodynamic critical point is gapless and whose finite-size gaps shrink with system size. The task will simulate anneals from a gapped paramagnet to the critical Hamiltonian and compare final excess energy density against ground-state fidelity under schedules with different endpoint smoothness/changing rates.

What the environment tests
  • Complete results are produced for all schedules, system sizes up to 20 qubits, and at least 8 runtime values per schedule.
  • The known qualitative Kibble-Zurek benchmark is reproduced: slower ramps reduce residual energy while final fidelity degrades with system size near the critical endpoint.
  • At least one regime is identified where the excess energy density is below 1e-2 per spin but fidelity is below 0.5 for 16 or more qubits, showing energy closeness without state closeness.
  • Schedule families are compared to test whether smoother, lower-rate schedules reduce excess energy at fixed runtime by at least 20% versus the linear schedule for any size at or above 14 qubits; otherwise a negative result with confidence intervals is reported.
  • Numerical accuracy is validated by showing the excess energy converges within 5% when the Trotter step count is doubled.
What it builds

A finite-size transverse-field Ising benchmark that Trotterizes real-time annealing from a gapped paramagnet toward the gapless critical point for open chains of 6 to 20 spins, under linear, cubic smoothstep, quintic smootherstep, and a gap-aware schedule. For each run it records the excess energy density, ground-state fidelity, energy variance, instantaneous minimum gap, and a schedule-roughness metric, then studies excess energy versus runtime, infidelity versus runtime, and finite-size scaling to locate regimes where the final energy is accurate even though the state is not.
